Fragrant flowering trees · Pennsylvania

Six fragrant trees, ranked by beauty

These are the fragrant trees we talked about, ordered by how striking they look in full bloom. The ranking is a matter of taste. None of them is on your community's prohibited list or the PA DCNR invasive list, but they aren't on the suggested list either, so ask the committee before you buy.

When each one blooms in PA

Choose two that bloom at different times and you'll have fragrance from April to September.

Star Magnolia

Magnolia stellata

For about two weeks in early spring, the bare branches are covered in white, star-shaped flowers, before any other tree has leafed out. It's the showiest tree on this list, and it stays compact enough for a front yard.

Watch for: a late frost can brown the open flowers. Planting it on the north or east side of the house delays bloom a little and protects it. Plant in spring, not fall.

White Fringetree

Chionanthus virginicus

In late spring the whole tree is draped in fluffy, ribbon-like white flowers, which give it the nickname "Grancy Graybeard." The scent is sweet and carries across the yard. It's native to the eastern U.S. and has yellow fall color.

Check with HOA: female trees produce dark blue berries that birds eat. Your list bans "Fruit Bearing Trees (all)," so ask for a male plant, which also has slightly showier flowers.

Japanese Tree Lilac 'Ivory Silk'

Syringa reticulata

Big, foamy cream-colored flower plumes cover the tree in June, after most spring bloomers have finished. It's very tough: it handles clay soil, road salt and drought, and it doesn't get the mildew that common lilac shrubs do. The bark is glossy reddish-brown, like a cherry's.

Good to know: the scent is strong and closer to privet than to a classic lilac. Most people like it, but a few find it musky. Smell one at a nursery in June if you can.

Yellowwood

Cladrastis kentukea

Long white flower chains hang from the branches like white wisteria, and they smell like it too. It also has smooth gray bark like a beech and clear yellow fall color. It would rank higher if it weren't so big.

Watch for: it blooms heavily only every second or third year, and it grows into a large shade tree. Choose it only if you have room to plant it 25 ft or more from the house.

Sweetbay Magnolia

Magnolia virginiana

It never puts on one big show, so it ranks lower here. But it's still my top overall pick for fragrance. Its creamy, lemon-scented flowers open a few at a time from May through summer, so you smell it for months. The leaves are silver underneath and shimmer in the wind, and they stay on through most PA winters.

Good to know: it's native to the Mid-Atlantic and one of the few trees that does well in wet or poorly drained soil. Plant in spring.

6

Seven-Son Flower

Heptacodium miconioides

The flowers are small, but it blooms in late summer when almost nothing else does. Its jasmine scent draws butterflies and bees. After the white flowers fall, the cups that held them turn rosy pink and look like a second bloom. In winter its peeling, tan bark stands out.

Good to know: it pairs well with Star Magnolia. One blooms first in spring and the other last in summer.
